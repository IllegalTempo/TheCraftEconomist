# Generated Citizen Households Design

Date: 2026-09-22
Status: Approved for implementation planning

## Purpose

Citizens should appear naturally around houses generated by The Economist. Each generated house starts one household of two to four Citizens. Its residents share a surname. This replaces the home-sign behavior and the expectation that a natural Citizen chooses an arbitrary unbuilt home spot. Player-created Citizens remain independent.

## Scope and success criteria

- Add one modest, recognizable house structure to new Overworld terrain. It can appear on suitable dry land in any Overworld biome with an acceptable site; proximity to water is not required. It does not generate in the Nether or End, in water, or on steep ground. It avoids known occupied structure sites where world generation exposes that information.
- Use a registered world structure and structure set rather than a runtime block-building loop. The default structure spacing is 24 chunks with separation 8 chunks; the data resources can be overridden by a data pack.
- Furnish the house with a door and four beds so its two to four initial residents have physical space. The first design is a small oak and stone house. No village or settlement system is added.
- Give each house a persistent household identity, anchor position, surname from the configured family-name list, and a fixed initial resident count of two to four. All natural residents from that house use that surname and have distinct given names when possible.
- Spawn the residents at safe, loaded positions immediately around the house when its anchor first becomes active on the server. A house is populated only once across normal saves and reloads. A resident's later death does not refill its slot.
- Natural residents use the house entrance as their saved home anchor and farm within the existing home work radius. They still prefer hydrated farmland when available and can use dry land if no watered site exists.
- Spawn eggs and `/summon` keep generating independent names and selecting their own home spots. Existing saved Citizens keep their names and saved homes.
- New Citizens receive no home sign. Citizens no longer place, replace, or manage signs. Existing placed signs stay as ordinary world blocks, and signs already in an old Citizen's inventory remain ordinary items.

## House world generation

The house is a single registered structure, backed by a structure template and a structure set. Placement samples the entire footprint during generation, requires solid reasonably level ground and an accessible dry entrance, and skips known occupied structure sites where that information is available. It does not force-load finished chunks to place a house. Invalid candidates are skipped rather than forcing a house into unsuitable terrain. The structure can be rotated with its entrance and household anchor transformed together.

Generation writes a stable house anchor that the server can discover when the anchor chunk loads. The server does not infer houses by scanning arbitrary player blocks. A house placed by a player or copied from the same palette does not automatically create Citizens. Existing explored chunks do not receive retroactive houses; houses appear as new terrain generates.

The house has enough light, a door, roof, floor, and four beds. It does not contain free valuables or a stocked farm. The template and world generation configuration are resources so a future data pack can replace them without changing Citizen identity logic.

## Household registry and spawning

A dimension-scoped saved registry is keyed by the generated house anchor. On first activation, it chooses the surname and resident count and stores them before issuing residents. Each resident slot has a stable house-derived UUID and a recorded issued state. The server looks for safe, empty spawn positions on the ground beside the house, never inside blocks or liquids. If a position is temporarily unavailable, that unissued slot can be retried when the house area loads again. An issued slot is never refilled after death or unloading.

World generation does not directly create entities. Population runs on the server when the anchor area is loaded, with bounded work and no forced chunk loading. The house registry and entity NBT both save the household anchor. Natural resident creation replaces only the random family name with the house surname; it retains the configured given-name generation, appearance, starter hoe, inventory, and other Citizen behavior. Reloading a resident preserves its existing identity and home rather than recomputing either from current config.

Normal save and reload must not create duplicate residents. A process crash between separate structure, registry, and entity saves is not guaranteed to be atomic; stable slot IDs and an issued-slot record favor avoiding duplicate Citizens, and a missing resident after such a crash is acceptable for this first slice.

## Existing Citizens and sign removal

The Citizen entity no longer inserts an oak sign in its starter inventory or runs the sign-facing and sign-placement tick path. The `HomeSignIssued` migration field becomes ignored on load and is no longer written. Previously saved signs in Citizen inventories are left untouched as normal items; already placed signs remain in the world. The old Citizen home-site chooser remains for spawn eggs, commands, and existing Citizens without a saved home. Existing saved homes are not moved to a generated house.

For a natural resident, the structure entrance is assigned as the saved home before its first farming tick. The home anchor is shared by residents, while each Citizen's farmland and inventory ownership remain independent. If the house is destroyed later, a surviving Citizen retains its saved home coordinate; rebuilding, migration, and eviction are outside this slice.

## Interfaces and failure handling

World generation owns only the house template and anchor. The household registry owns one-time issuance and surname selection. A population service reads loaded house anchors and asks the Citizen factory to create a resident for a specific slot and surname. The Citizen entity owns its persisted identity, home, farming state, and inventory. The spawn egg path never queries the household registry.

The service skips a house if the chunk is unloading or there is no safe spawn tile. It retries only unissued slots later. It does not spawn through walls, in water, or in unloaded chunks. An invalid or missing family-name configuration continues to use the existing config fallback behavior. Multiple houses can coincidentally choose the same surname from a short configured list; the guarantee is that residents of one house match, not that all houses have unique surnames.

## Verification

- Unit tests cover stable household IDs, surname assignment, two-to-four resident counts, one-time issuance, and persistence across registry reloads.
- Game tests cover initial population near a house anchor, shared surnames, distinct spawn-egg naming behavior, saved home anchors, no replacement after death, and no sign item or placement.
- A fresh-world manual check confirms that the structure appears on suitable Overworld land, the entrance and beds are usable, residents spawn nearby, and reloading does not add residents. Existing-world checks confirm saved Citizens retain names and homes and old placed signs remain.

## Out of scope

There is no family relationship simulation, marriage, birth, respawning population, multiple house styles, village integration, player-built house recognition, relocation, retroactive structure placement, or automatic reassignment of old Citizens.
